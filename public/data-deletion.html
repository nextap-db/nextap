Data requests are handled manually through NexTap support. Account ownership may need to be checked before information is shared or changed.

×Request deletion

There is no self-service account deletion or data export button. These links open an email draft; you choose whether to send it.

02What to include

  1. Name the request. Say whether you need an update, deactivation, deletion, or access to a copy.
  2. Identify the record. Include your name, account email, and profile link if available. For order information, include the order reference.
  3. Explain the scope. State the details to change or which account, profile, order, or other information your request concerns.

Send the request to nextapph@gmail.com. You can ask for help choosing the appropriate request.

03Ownership checks

Share enough to verify the request, not your secrets.

Never include your account password, one-time codes, or full payment credentials in an email.

NexTap may request reasonable additional information to check that you own the account or are authorized to act for its owner. Only information needed for that check should be provided through the confirmed support channel.

If you have lost account access, mention that in the request. Support can also help with manual password reset and activation.

04What happens after a request

NexTap reviews the request, checks ownership where needed, and determines the records and actions involved. Timing depends on verification, the request’s scope, and any applicable obligations.

A profile update changes the available fields. Deactivation stops the current public profile from displaying and makes client sign-in and existing sessions unavailable while the account is inactive. Where deletion is appropriate, support can remove the client record and review related information separately.

Deleting a client record does not automatically remove separate orders, provider notifications, retained copies, or backups. Include those records in your request when relevant so their treatment can be reviewed.

05Retention, backups, and public copies

Erasure and blocking rights apply under the conditions in Philippine privacy law. Some information may remain necessary for a legal obligation, fulfilment, security, or a legal claim. Any retained information should be limited to its lawful purpose.

Deletion requests should address relevant live records and retained copies or backups where applicable. NexTap can explain what is covered, what remains necessary, and why. Client and order records are handled manually rather than removed through an automatic purge.

An NFC card’s printed artwork cannot be withdrawn remotely. Disabling the profile affects its link, card, and QR access. NexTap cannot guarantee removal of screenshots, downloads, or copies held by other people or services; search results and third-party copies may need separate removal requests. Ask NexTap to consider recipient deletion or search delisting requests where applicable.

Read the National Privacy Commission’s guidance on erasure and blocking for the conditions and limits.

06Access, portability, and privacy concerns

You can request access to personal information about you. Where applicable, data portability lets you obtain an electronic copy in a commonly used, structured format. Ownership checks and safeguards for other people’s information can apply.

For a question or concern, contact NexTap support. You may also use the National Privacy Commission’s complaint process. Its data subject rights page explains the available rights and links to complaint guidance.

See the Privacy Policy for information handling and the Account Security page for access help. The implementing rules of the Data Privacy Act provide the legal framework.
